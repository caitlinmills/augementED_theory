Conceptual Theory Buckets

Field Reference for Mapping Teacher Stories to Learning Science

Purpose: Rapid orientation during teacher stories. This page answers: What kind of learning problem is this?

The Six Buckets

Mental Representations & Knowledge Networks

Understanding depends on how ideas are connected, not how many are known.

Listen For

  • "They know the pieces but can't put it together."
  • "They memorized it but don't really get it."
  • "They can do it one way but can't transfer."

Listen For

  • "They know the pieces but can't put it together."
  • "They memorized it but don't really get it."
  • "They can do it one way but can't transfer."

Mechanistic Breakdowns

  • Fragmented schemas
  • Weak causal structure
  • Surface-level encoding
  • Representation misalignment (text vs. diagram vs. talk)

Attention, Working Memory & Cognitive Load

Learning is constrained by what learners attend to and can coordinate at once.

Listen For

  • "They're overwhelmed."
  • "They miss the important part."
  • "They focus on the wrong thing."

Listen For

  • "They're overwhelmed."
  • "They miss the important part."
  • "They focus on the wrong thing."

Mechanistic Breakdowns

  • Misdirected attention
  • Extraneous cognitive load
  • Split attention
  • Prior knowledge not activated

Inference, Explanation & Reasoning

Understanding grows through generating, testing, and revising inferences.

Listen For

  • "They wait for the answer."
  • "They can't explain why."
  • "Discussion stays surface-level."

Listen For

  • "They wait for the answer."
  • "They can't explain why."
  • "Discussion stays surface-level."

Mechanistic Breakdowns

  • Low inference demand
  • Explanations without mechanisms
  • Weak counterfactual reasoning
  • No productive failure opportunities

Monitoring, Feedback & Self-Regulation

Learning improves when learners can detect and respond to gaps in understanding.

Listen For

  • "They don't use feedback."
  • "They think they get it, then bomb the test."
  • "They give up or keep making the same mistake."

Listen For

  • "They don't use feedback."
  • "They think they get it, then bomb the test."
  • "They give up or keep making the same mistake."

Mechanistic Breakdowns

  • Poor metacognitive calibration
  • Feedback that evaluates but doesn't inform
  • No visibility into progress
  • Weak error detection

Epistemic Stance, Affect & Identity

Engagement deepens when learners see uncertainty as productive rather than threatening.

Listen For

  • "They're afraid to be wrong."
  • "They just want the right answer."
  • "They shut down when it gets hard."

Listen For

  • "They're afraid to be wrong."
  • "They just want the right answer."
  • "They shut down when it gets hard."

Mechanistic Breakdowns

  • Fixed view of knowledge
  • High epistemic risk
  • Confusion interpreted as failure
  • Threatened learner identity

Collaborative Sensemaking & Distributed Cognition

Understanding can be built jointly when ideas are visible and taken up by others.

Listen For

  • "Group work is busy but shallow."
  • "One person does all the thinking."
  • "Ideas don't build on each other."

Listen For

  • "Group work is busy but shallow."
  • "One person does all the thinking."
  • "Ideas don't build on each other."

Mechanistic Breakdowns

  • No shared representations
  • Low dialogic uptake
  • Uneven participation in reasoning
  • Missing transactive exchange
